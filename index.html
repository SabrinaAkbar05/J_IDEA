The Experience

Two Days.
Two Challenges.

Each day tests a different dimension of your HCI knowledge — from critical evaluation to creative prototyping under pressure.

Day One

HCI Project Evaluation

Present your existing or newly crafted HCI project to a panel of judges. Demonstrate your understanding of user-centred design principles, accessibility standards, and usability heuristics.

  • Present your HCI project to judges
  • Demonstrate usability thinking
  • Discuss accessibility compliance
  • Answer Q&A on design decisions
  • Teams of 1–2 participants
Day Two

Live Figma Design Challenge

A prototype is revealed on the spot. You and your partner have a fixed time window to recreate and improve upon it using Figma — then defend every design choice to the jury.

  • Blind prototype reveal at start
  • Design using Figma only
  • AI assistance allowed (with disclosure)
  • Live evaluation: usability & rationale
  • Max team size: 2 persons
Official Rulebook

Know the Rules.
Win with Intent.

01
🤖

AI Tools — Allowed

Generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Copilot, Midjourney, etc.) are permitted as design aids. However, all AI usage must be disclosed to judges. Final explanations and rationale must come from the team — not AI output.

02
👥

Team Size — Max 2

Teams may consist of 1 or 2 participants. Groups of 3 or more are not permitted. Solo entries are welcome. All team members must be registered individually before the event.

03
🖼️

Prototype Challenge

On Day 2, a reference prototype will be unveiled at the start of the session. Teams must design a similar prototype in Figma within the allotted time. No pre-made components may be imported unless specified.

04
🗣️

Evaluation & Defence

All participants must verbally explain their design decisions. Judges will ask questions on usability heuristics, accessibility, user flows, and visual hierarchy. Silent submissions will be penalised.

05
♿

Accessibility Requirements

Submissions must demonstrate awareness of WCAG 2.1 guidelines: colour contrast, readable font sizes, keyboard navigation consideration, and inclusive design principles. These are judged separately.

06
⏱️

Conduct & Fair Play

Plagiarism or copying another team's work results in immediate disqualification. Judges' decisions are final. All participants must maintain respectful conduct toward peers, judges, and organisers.

Evaluation Framework

How You'll
Be Judged

Scoring is based on five pillars that reflect the core competencies of HCI professionals.

  • 25%
    Usability & Heuristics

    Does the design follow Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics? Is navigation intuitive? Are errors prevented and recoverable?

  • 20%
    Visual Design & Consistency

    Cohesive colour palette, typography system, spacing, and component consistency throughout the prototype.

  • 20%
    Accessibility Compliance

    Colour contrast ratios, text legibility, inclusive design considerations, and WCAG awareness demonstrated.

  • 20%
    Verbal Defence & Rationale

    Ability to articulate design decisions, respond to judge questions, and demonstrate HCI knowledge during evaluation.

  • 15%
    Innovation & Creativity

    Unique interaction patterns, thoughtful micro-interactions, and going beyond the reference prototype in meaningful ways.

Questions

Frequently Asked

Can I participate solo? +
Yes! Solo participants are welcome. The maximum is 2 per team — forming a pair gives you a collaborative advantage on Day 2, but solo entries are judged on the same criteria.
Do I need prior Figma experience? +
A working knowledge of Figma is highly recommended for Day 2. You should be comfortable with frames, auto-layout, components, and basic prototyping. Figma's free tier is sufficient — no Pro plan required.
Which AI tools are allowed? +
Any AI tool is permitted as a design aid (ChatGPT, Copilot, Uizard, Galileo AI, etc.). However, you must disclose all AI usage to the judges and be able to explain every design decision yourself. Copying AI output verbatim without understanding it is penalised.
What should Day 1 participants prepare? +
Bring a completed HCI project (could be a prototype, design system, research study, or usability evaluation). You'll present it to judges who will ask questions on design rationale, usability, and accessibility. A Figma or PDF walkthrough is recommended.
Is there a registration fee? +
Registration details including fees will be communicated after form submission via your provided email. Check back on the website for updates.
How are winners decided? +
Judges score each team across five dimensions: Usability, Visual Design, Accessibility, Verbal Defence, and Creativity (see the Judging Criteria section). Scores from both days are combined for final rankings.

J-IDEA Official Rulebook

Edition 2026 — HCI Competition

🤖 Rule 1 — AI Tool Usage

Generative AI tools are permitted as design aids during both days. This includes but is not limited to ChatGPT, Midjourney, Uizard, Galileo AI, and GitHub Copilot. All AI usage must be verbally disclosed to judges before your evaluation begins. Participants must be able to independently explain all design decisions and demonstrate comprehension. AI output presented as original thought is considered academic dishonesty and results in disqualification.

👥 Rule 2 — Team Composition

Teams consist of 1 or 2 participants only. Groups of 3 or more are strictly not allowed. All team members must be currently enrolled university students and must individually register prior to the event deadline. Team members cannot be swapped after registration is confirmed.

🖼️ Rule 3 — Day 2 Prototype Challenge

At the commencement of Day 2, a reference prototype (a UI screen or flow) will be displayed to all participants simultaneously. Teams must recreate and where possible improve upon this prototype using Figma only. External UI kits and pre-built component libraries are not permitted unless explicitly announced. Teams must work only within Figma during the design window.

🗣️ Rule 4 — Verbal Evaluation

All participants must verbally present and defend their design to the judging panel. Judges will ask questions based on: Nielsen's Usability Heuristics, WCAG accessibility guidelines, Gestalt principles, user flow logic, and visual design rationale. Teams that submit work but do not engage in verbal evaluation receive a zero for the Defence criterion.

♿ Rule 5 — Accessibility Standards

All designs should demonstrate awareness of WCAG 2.1 Level AA guidelines. Key checks include: minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio for body text, scalable font sizes, logical tab order, and inclusive design patterns. Judges will specifically probe on accessibility decisions made (or omitted) in your prototype.

⚖️ Rule 6 — General Conduct

Plagiarism, copying, or directly downloading another team's Figma file results in immediate disqualification. Participants must maintain professional conduct at all times. Late submissions after the design window closes will not be accepted. Judges' decisions are final and binding. Organizers reserve the right to disqualify any team for rule violations.

📋 Rule 7 — Submission Requirements

Day 1: Share a Figma link or PDF of your HCI project with organisers at least 24 hours before presentation. Day 2: Submit your Figma file link in the provided submission form immediately when the design window ends. Submissions must include at minimum: a working prototype, a component overview frame, and a brief annotation of key design decisions.
